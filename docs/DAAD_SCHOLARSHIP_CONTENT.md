## DAAD/EPOS Scholarship Description (Chevening Format)

The DAAD/EPOS Scholarship is Germany's flagship development scholarship, funding young professionals from developing countries to pursue specialized master's degrees that drive sustainable change in their home regions.

Since 1961, DAAD has supported over 2 million scholars worldwide, creating a powerful network of leaders across government, industry, and civil society in 160+ countries.

### What Makes DAAD/EPOS Unique

- Target development-focused master's programmes at top German universities
- Join a 60-year legacy of development practitioners and change-makers
- Access Germany's world-class research infrastructure and faculty
- Return home with specialized skills directly applicable to your field

### What's Covered

Monthly stipend (€850-1,200), full health insurance, return airfare, and study allowance. Admin fees covered for most programmes. You join a lifetime alumni network across sectors.

### Application Timeline 2027/28

- **June 15, 2026:** Application period opens
- **October 15, 2026:** Applications close
- **December 2026 - March 2027:** Programme committee selection + DAAD review
- **April 2027:** Final scholarship decisions announced
- **October 2027:** Studies begin

### Who Can Apply (MSc Environmental Governance Example)

**You qualify if you:**

- Hold a bachelor's degree (awarded 2020-2026, upper third of class)
- Have 2+ years relevant professional experience (post-bachelor, not academic/teaching)
- Are from a DAC developing country
- Have lived in Germany for ≤15 months total
- Can demonstrate clear development impact goals

**Note:** Already have a master's? You'll need strong justification for a second postgraduate degree.

### How to Win a DAAD/EPOS Scholarship

**Strong candidates demonstrate:**

- Specific, measurable professional achievements in development work
- Clear gap between current skills and impact goals
- Direct connection between chosen programme and home country needs
- Concrete post-study implementation plan with timeline

**Common mistakes:**

- Vague "I want to help my country" statements
- Academic/teaching experience instead of practitioner work
- Applying with degree older than 6 years
- Missing development-relevance connection
- Not addressing why they need THIS specific programme

### Application Checklist (MSc Environmental Governance)

1. Complete Online Eligibility Assessment (OEA)
2. Compile all documents into ONE PDF:
   - Motivation letter (development-focused)
   - CV (Europass format)
   - University transcripts + degree certificate
   - Language certificate (IELTS/TOEFL or German)
   - Professional references (sent separately by referees to info-meg@unr.uni-freiburg.de)
   - Proof of work experience (2+ years)
3. Email complete PDF to info-meg@unr.uni-freiburg.de
4. Ensure referees send letters directly (not through you)

**Pro Tip:** DAAD selects "development practitioners," not just high achievers. Show measurable impact: "I manage watershed restoration covering 8,000 hectares serving 12 villages" beats "I am passionate about environmental conservation." Every sentence should connect your past work, current gap, and future implementation plan.

## YouTube Video Script (8-10 minutes)

**SCENE 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:30)**

Germany funds more international students than almost any other country in the world. One program alone — DAAD — has been doing it for over 70 years. In this video, I'll break down exactly how it works, who qualifies, what the application actually needs, and what separates funded candidates from rejected ones.

---

**SCENE 2 — WHAT IS DAAD (0:30–1:30)**

DAAD stands for Deutscher Akademischer Austauschdienst — the German Academic Exchange Service. It's funded by the German government and the European Union.

Every year they fund over 100,000 people across scholarships, research grants, and institutional partnerships.

For master's students from developing countries, the main programs are:
• EPOS — for development practitioners
• Helmut-Schmidt — for public administration
• Research Grants — for PhD and postdoc work
• In-Country/In-Region — study within your region

We're focusing on EPOS today because that's the most relevant for most viewers.

---

**SCENE 3 — WHAT'S COVERED (1:30–3:00)**

Here's exactly what DAAD covers:

• Monthly stipend between €850 and €1,200 depending on your degree level
• Full health, accident, and liability insurance
• Return flight to Germany and back
• Study and research allowance
• Partial or full tuition depending on the program

One important note: unlike Chevening, DAAD doesn't always cover 100% of tuition. Most public German universities charge €1,500–3,000 per semester in admin fees, not actual tuition. DAAD covers those.

The stipend alone — €1,000/month — is enough to live comfortably in most German cities outside Munich.

---

**SCENE 4 — WHO QUALIFIES (3:00–4:30)**

DAAD is specifically designed for students from developing countries.

You need:
• A completed university degree — bachelor's minimum
• Strong academic record — roughly 70% or above equivalent
• Professional experience is strongly preferred for EPOS
• Citizenship of a DAAD-eligible country — which includes most of Africa, Asia, and Latin America
• A clear connection between your work and development goals

The last point is critical and most people miss it. DAAD is not selecting the smartest applicants. They are selecting people who will go back home and use this degree to solve real problems.

If your application doesn't show that, it doesn't matter how strong your grades are.

---

**SCENE 5 — WHAT ACTUALLY WINS (4:30–6:00)**

Let me show you the difference between a weak and a strong application.

Weak motivation letter opening:
"I have always been passionate about development and I want to use my skills to help my community."

Strong motivation letter opening:
"I currently manage irrigation infrastructure covering 8,000 hectares across three districts in Ethiopia. The water loss rate in our system is 34% above acceptable levels. This program's hydraulic engineering module is the specific training I need to redesign the system and reduce that loss by 2028."

See the difference? The second one has numbers, a specific problem, and a direct link to the program.

Every sentence in your motivation letter should answer: what problem am I solving, what have I already done about it, and what will this degree let me do next?

---

**SCENE 6 — APPLICATION TIMELINE (6:00–7:00)**

Here's the timeline you need to work backward from:

• October 2026 — Most DAAD applications open
• November to December 2026 — Submission deadlines (these vary by program, so check your specific one)
• February to March 2027 — Selection results
• April 2027 — Acceptance deadline
• September or October 2027 — Studies begin

One thing most applicants miss: DAAD requires your home institution or employer to nominate you for some programs. Check if your program needs this before the deadline — it takes time to arrange.

---

**SCENE 7 — DOCUMENTS CHECKLIST (7:00–8:00)**

Here's everything you need:

✓ Motivation letter — 2 pages maximum, development-focused
✓ Europass CV — that's the European format, link in description
✓ Two references — academic or professional
✓ University transcripts plus your degree certificate
✓ Language certificate — IELTS, TOEFL, or German equivalent
✓ Statement of purpose tied specifically to development goals

One extra thing: some programs require a project outline or research proposal. Check the requirements for your specific program early.
