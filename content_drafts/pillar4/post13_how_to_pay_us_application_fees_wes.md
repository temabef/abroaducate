One of the most frustrating experiences during the international admissions process occurs at the very final step: you spend months drafting Statements of Purpose, studying for standardized tests, requesting recommendation letters, and filling out university portals, only for your local debit card to be repeatedly declined on the checkout page.

This issue is an everyday reality for prospective students across Africa, South Asia, Latin America, and parts of the Middle East.

Central banks and domestic commercial banks in countries like Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Pakistan, and Egypt frequently impose severe restrictions on foreign currency transactions. Many domestic debit cards carry strict monthly international spending limits of **$20 to $50**, or have international online payments disabled completely.

When a university application fee costs $85, a WES credential evaluation costs $220, or the US SEVIS I-901 fee costs $350, standard domestic bank cards fail immediately with generic errors such as *"Transaction Declined," "Do Not Honor,"* or *"Processor Network Error."*

Below, we explain why these transactions fail, how billing address mismatches trigger anti-fraud blocks, and the step-by-step solution to pay all US university fees using virtual multi-currency cards.

## 1. Why US University and WES Payments Fail

![Close-up flatlay of smartphone displaying successful virtual card payment next to laptop with university portal confirmation]({{BODY_IMAGE_URL}})

Understanding why your payment is blocked is essential to resolving it:

1. **Foreign Exchange Rationing & Card Caps:** Local commercial banks restrict foreign currency outflows. If a bank imposes a $20 monthly international limit, any attempt to pay an $85 application fee is blocked automatically at the host bank level.
2. **AVS (Address Verification System) Mismatches:** US payment gateways (such as Stripe, CyberSource, and Authorize.Net) cross-reference the billing address entered on the university form against the registered billing address of the credit card. If an international card does not support digital AVS verification, the gateway rejects the transaction to prevent fraud.
3. **High-Risk Country Geofencing:** Certain US payment processors automatically flag IP addresses originating from specific foreign regions, resulting in automated security blocks regardless of card balances.
