To obtain a National Student Visa (Nationalvisum § 16b AufenthG) for Germany, every international student from outside the European Union must provide proof of financial resources (*Finanzierungsnachweis*).

For the vast majority of international applicants, this requirement is fulfilled by opening a **German Blocked Account (*Sperrkonto*)**.

A blocked account is a specialized bank deposit where students deposit their living funds for the entire academic year. The German Federal Foreign Office sets the minimum statutory amount: currently **€11,904 per year (€992 per month)**. Once in Germany, the bank disburses exactly €992 each month into your local current account, guaranteeing you have sufficient funds to cover rent, food, and living expenses.

In the German student visa market, two digital providers dominate: **Expatrio** and **Fintiba**.

Both providers are officially approved by the German Federal Foreign Office (*Auswärtiges Amt*). Both allow you to open an account online from your home country before booking your visa appointment.

However, key differences exist in setup fees, monthly account maintenance charges, health insurance bundling, customer support speed, and initial cashback bonuses.

Below, we provide an objective, side-by-side comparison of Expatrio and Fintiba to help you choose the best provider for your German student visa in 2027.

## 4. Bank Account Activation & Current Account Integration

Once you arrive in Germany, you must unblock your funds by opening a local German checking account (*Girokonto*):

- **With Expatrio:** The Expatrio app provides an integrated digital current account powered by European partner banks. You can activate your monthly payout directly within the Expatrio app without visiting a physical bank branch or waiting weeks for postal PIN codes.
- **With Fintiba:** Fintiba acts purely as a blocked account trustee. Upon arrival, you must independently find a third-party German bank (such as N26, Deutsche Bank, or Sparkasse), open a Girokonto, and link its IBAN back into the Fintiba portal to initiate your monthly transfers.

## 5. Transferring Blocked Account Funds from Overseas

Depositing €11,904 into a German bank account from outside Europe can be complicated by local exchange controls, arbitrary banking restrictions, and high wire transfer markups.

Traditional international bank wires often pass through multiple intermediary correspondent banks, which can deduct hidden fees along the way and cause your deposit to arrive short of the statutory €11,904 requirement. If your deposit is even €10 short, your blocked account confirmation document (*Sperrbestätigung*) cannot be generated.
