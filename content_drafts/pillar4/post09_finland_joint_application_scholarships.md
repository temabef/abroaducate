Finland consistently ranks as the happiest nation in the world, renowned globally for its technological innovation, societal safety, and world-class educational infrastructure.

For international students outside the European Union and European Economic Area (non-EU/EEA), Finnish universities present one of the most generous and streamlined postgraduate scholarship ecosystems in Europe.

Unlike decentralized admissions systems where applicants must research and apply to individual university portals separately, Finland operates a centralized national admissions system known as the **Joint Application (Yhteishaku)** via the official portal, **Studyinfo.fi**.

Through this single application portal, international applicants can apply to up to **six degree programs** across different Finnish universities in one unified submission—and simultaneously be considered for **100% tuition fee waiver scholarships** and the prestigious national **Finland Scholarship**.

However, the application window is extremely brief: the Joint Application for English-taught Master's and Bachelor's programs opens in **early January** and closes strictly in **mid-January** each year.

Below, we detail how the Finnish Joint Application works, the scholarship schemes available at top universities, document requirements, and step-by-step submission instructions.

---

## 1. How the Finnish Joint Application Works

The Joint Application is a centralized digital admission system coordinated by the Finnish National Agency for Education (OPH):

- **Single Application Dossier:** You complete one master application form on Studyinfo.fi.
- **Preference Hierarchy:** You select up to **six degree programs** across Finland, ranking them in order of academic preference (1 to 6).
- **Single Scholarship Tick-Box:** At virtually every Finnish university, you do not need to submit a separate scholarship dossier. In the joint application form, there is a simple question: *"Do you wish to apply for an institutional tuition fee waiver scholarship?"* Checking "Yes" automatically routes your academic profile to the university's scholarship selection committee.
- **Strict Timelines:** Applications open on **January 7, 2027** and close on **January 21, 2027** at 15:00 Finnish time (EET). Missing this two-week window means waiting an entire year for the next intake.

## 2. Types of Scholarships Available at Finnish Universities

![Scandinavian oak desk flatlay with Studyinfo portal on laptop, transcripts, notebook, and coffee]({{BODY_IMAGE_URL}})

Non-EU/EEA international students admitted to Finnish universities are subject to tuition fees ranging from **€8,000 to €18,000 per year**. However, top-ranked institutions award extensive merit waivers:

### 1. The Finland Scholarship (Suomi-stipendi)
Funded by the Finnish Ministry of Education and Culture, this elite national scholarship is awarded to the highest-scoring international Master's applicants at Finnish research universities:
- **100% Tuition Fee Waiver:** Covers full tuition for the first year of the Master's program.
- **€5,000 Relocation Grant:** A one-time non-repayable cash stipend deposited into the student's Finnish bank account upon arrival to assist with initial living and accommodation costs.
- **Second-Year Renewal:** Automatic tuition waiver extension upon completing a minimum of 55 ECTS credits during your first academic year.

### 2. University Institutional Tuition Fee Waivers
Every Finnish university maintains independent merit waiver quotas:
- **Aalto University:** Awards 100% and 50% tuition fee waivers across the School of Science, School of Engineering, and School of Business.
- **University of Helsinki:** Offers full 100% tuition waivers plus annual €10,000 student living grants to outstanding scholars.
- **Tampere University:** Generous Tampere University Tuition Fee Scholarships (100% tuition waiver for the entire duration of the degree).
- **LUT University (Lappeenranta-Lahti):** Known for sustainability and software technology, LUT awards 100% tuition scholarships covering both years of Master's study to high-merit candidates.
- **University of Oulu:** Offers international scholarship discounts reducing tuition by up to 70% to 100% across ICT, architecture, and engineering.

## 3. Required Application Documents Checklist

Finnish admissions committees evaluate applicants based purely on documented academic qualifications:

1. **Official Bachelor's Degree Certificate:** Scanned copies of your original degree certificate or a provisional certificate of graduation.
2. **Comprehensive Academic Transcripts:** Full semester mark sheets detailing course titles, grades, and credit hours or ECTS equivalents.
3. **Certified Translations:** If your academic records were issued in any language other than English, Finnish, or Swedish, you must provide certified, word-for-word translations accompanied by an official translator's signature and stamp. You can order certified academic translations meeting Finnish university and immigration criteria through [ImmiTranslate](https://immitranslate.com/?ref=543dj3rd).
4. **English Language Proficiency Proof:** Accepted standardized tests include IELTS Academic (minimum 6.5 overall, 5.5 in writing) or TOEFL iBT (minimum 92, 22 in writing). Some universities also accept PTE Academic or C1 Advanced.
5. **Motivation Letter / Statement of Purpose:** Some specific programs require a 1 to 2-page essay answering program-specific prompts regarding previous research and career goals. You can draft and refine your motivation letter using the [Abroaducate SOP Builder](/sop).
6. **Curriculum Vitae (CV):** Formatted in Europass style or clean academic layout detailing academic research, software skills, and internships.

## 4. Student Residence Permit & Living Cost Requirements

Finland is known for affordable student housing coordinated through student housing foundations like HOAS (Helsinki), TOAS (Tampere), and PSOAS (Oulu). Monthly student living expenses typically range from **€700 to €900**, including housing, food, and public transport.

### The Finnish Residence Permit Financial Requirement
To secure a Finnish student residence permit (Type A continuous permit) from the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri):
- You must demonstrate proof of funds of at least **€560 per month** (€6,720 for a one-year permit or €13,440 for a two-year permit) in a bank account held in your own name.
- You must possess comprehensive international student health insurance covering medical expenses up to €120,000.
- International students can secure verified statutory-equivalent health insurance packages compliant with Migri residence permit standards through [Expatrio](https://www.expatrio.com?p=abroaducate123).
