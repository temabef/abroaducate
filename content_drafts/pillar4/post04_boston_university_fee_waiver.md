Boston University (BU) is one of the premier private research institutions in the United States, home to world-class programs across the Graduate School of Arts & Sciences (GRS), College of Engineering (ENG), Questrom School of Business, and School of Public Health.

However, applying to graduate study at BU involves an application fee of **$85 to $95** per program. Because many international candidates apply to multiple schools or multiple departments, this initial investment represents a substantial hurdle.

Fortunately, Boston University maintains several legitimate avenues through which international and domestic prospective students can obtain a full application fee waiver.

In this guide, we break down how fee waivers operate across BU's major colleges, how to attend virtual preview days to secure waiver vouchers, and the exact steps to submit your application with a $0 balance.

---

## 1. How Fee Waivers Work Across BU Graduate Colleges

Unlike universities with a single centralized admissions bureau, graduate admissions at Boston University are decentralized. Each college within BU sets its own fee waiver policies:

### Graduate School of Arts & Sciences (GRS)
The Graduate School of Arts & Sciences—which houses Computer Science, Economics, Biology, Physics, and Mathematics—provides application fee waivers primarily through:
- **Affiliated Pipeline Programs:** Active participants or alumni of programs like GEM, McNair Scholars, Gates Millennium Scholars, and SACNAS.
- **Economic Hardship Requests:** International students experiencing severe documented financial distress or foreign currency transfer bans may submit an institutional fee waiver request directly to the GRS Admissions Office before submitting their dossier.

### College of Engineering (ENG)
The College of Engineering (ECE, Mechanical, Biomedical, Materials Science) is among the most generous colleges at BU for recruitment waivers:
- **Virtual Graduate Open House Events:** In the autumn and early winter, BU ENG hosts virtual graduate preview sessions. Registered attendees who participate in the live webinars often receive a **promotional waiver code** or have their application fees waived automatically upon submission.
- **Engineering Discovery Days:** Prospective MS and PhD students who attend designated faculty panel discussions are eligible for priority application waivers.

### Questrom School of Business
For specialized Master of Science programs (such as the MS in Business Analytics or MS in Mathematical Finance), Questrom offers fee waivers to attendees of specific virtual admissions workshops, military veterans, and corporate partner candidates.

## 2. Earning Fee Waivers Through BU Virtual Admissions Events

![Study desk flatlay with Boston University application documents, transcript scans, notebook, and coffee]({{BODY_IMAGE_URL}})

For most international applicants without US pipeline program affiliations, the single most dependable method to earn a BU fee waiver is attending a **Virtual Admissions Preview Session**:

1. **Monitor Event Calendars:** In September, October, and November, visit the admissions events page for your specific BU target school (e.g., BU College of Engineering Graduate Admissions).
2. **Register with Your Primary Email:** Always register using the exact email address you will use when creating your application on BU's Liaison CAS application portal.
3. **Participate Actively:** Attend the scheduled webinar from beginning to end. Graduate admissions coordinators typically track attendance timestamps.
4. **Collect Your Code:** The alphanumeric coupon code is either shared on the final slide of the webinar or sent via email to verified attendees within 48 to 72 hours following the event.

## 3. Step-by-Step: Entering Your Waiver in the BU Application Portal

Most graduate programs at Boston University use customized instances of the **Liaison CAS (Centralized Application Service)** portal (e.g., BU GRS CAS or BU ENG CAS).

Follow these steps to ensure your waiver applies correctly:

1. **Complete All Program Sections:** Fill in your personal information, academic coursework history, resume, and statement of purpose.
2. **Assign Letters of Recommendation:** Provide contact information for your referees so that automated recommendation links are dispatched.
3. **Navigate to the Submit Application Tab:** Review your checklist to ensure all required fields are marked with a green checkmark.
4. **Enter Your Coupon Code:** On the payment screen, locate the field labeled **Coupon Code** or **Waiver Code**. Enter your verified code exactly as provided and click **Apply**.
5. **Confirm $0 Balance:** The submission fee must reflect **$0.00**. Only then should you click **Submit**. Never pay the fee with a credit card expecting a retroactive refund; BU admissions offices do not reimburse previously paid application fees under any circumstance.

## 4. Key Application Materials & Preparation

When applying to Boston University with a fee waiver, your academic credentials must still meet the highest evaluative standards:

1. **Certified Transcripts & Credentials:** International applicants must provide complete scanned transcripts for all post-secondary coursework. If documents were issued in a language other than English, certified word-for-word translations are required. You can order certified academic translations meeting US admissions standards via [ImmiTranslate](https://immitranslate.com/?ref=543dj3rd).
2. **Statement of Purpose (SOP):** A rigorous 2-page essay explaining your technical background, specific faculty members whose research aligns with your interests, and your post-graduation career plan. Structure and polish your statement using the [Abroaducate SOP Builder](/sop).
3. **Letters of Recommendation:** 2 to 3 strong letters from academic advisors or senior managers detailing your research abilities and analytical maturity.
4. **Curriculum Vitae (CV):** Highlighting research internships, software proficiencies, publications, and capstone projects.
5. **English Language Proficiency Scores:** Accepted tests include TOEFL iBT (minimum typically 90 to 100 depending on department) or IELTS (minimum 7.0).

## 5. Budgeting for Boston & Managing Living Costs

Boston is an academic hub, but it is also one of the more expensive metropolitan areas in North America. International students should budget between **$1,500 and $2,200 per month** for off-campus accommodation, food, utilities, and public transit.

Admitted doctoral students typically receive full tuition scholarships plus research or teaching stipends. However, MS students must show proof of funds for tuition and living expenses during the I-20 visa stage.
