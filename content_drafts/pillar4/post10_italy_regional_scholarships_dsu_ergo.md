Italy is home to some of the oldest and most prestigious universities in the Western world, including the University of Bologna, Sapienza University of Rome, Politecnico di Milano, and the University of Padua.

While Italian public universities already maintain remarkably affordable base tuition fees (typically ranging from €1,000 to €3,000 per academic year), Italy offers one of the most generous financial aid systems in the world for international students: **Regional Right-to-Study Scholarships (Borse di Studio Regionali)**.

Every administrative region in Italy operates a dedicated Right-to-Study agency:
- **DSU (Diritto allo Studio Universitario):** Tuscany (Pisa, Florence, Siena) and Lombardy (Milan).
- **ER.GO (Azienda Regionale per il Diritto agli Studi Superiori):** Emilia-Romagna (Bologna, Modena, Parma, Ferrara).
- **EDISU:** Piedmont (Politecnico di Torino, University of Turin).
- **LazioDisco:** Lazio (Sapienza University of Rome, Tor Vergata, Roma Tre).
- **ALiSEO:** Liguria (University of Genoa).

These scholarships are **not based purely on academic test scores or first-class honors**. Instead, they are constitutional, income-assessed grants designed to guarantee equal access to higher education for economically disadvantaged students worldwide.

Below, we detail what these regional scholarships provide, the eligibility thresholds, how the critical **ISEE Parificato** financial certificate is calculated, and the step-by-step roadmap to secure full funding.

---

## 1. What the Italian Regional Scholarship Covers

A successful regional scholarship award transforms studying in Italy into a fully funded degree:

1. **100% Tuition Fee Exemption:** Complete waiver of university enrollment tuition fees (students pay only the regional stamp tax of approximately €140 to €160).
2. **Annual Living Cash Stipend:** Non-EU students residing away from home (classified as *"studenti fuori sede"*) receive a cash stipend ranging between **€6,800 and €8,500 per year**, disbursed in two installments.
3. **Subsidized Student Housing:** Guaranteed placement in student residence halls (collegi universitari) or an additional rent reimbursement allowance if university housing is unavailable.
4. **Free Daily Cafeteria Meals:** One to two free balanced meals every day at university canteens (mense universitarie) across campus.

## 2. Eligibility & The ISEE Parificato Explained

![Study table flatlay with Italian scholarship documentation, apostilled tax records, and espresso cup]({{BODY_IMAGE_URL}})

Regional scholarships are awarded based on an economic indicator called the **ISEE Parificato** (Indicatore della Situazione Economica Equivalente for foreign students).

The ISEE Parificato measures the total economic condition of your family household based on three factors:
- **Annual Family Income:** Gross income earned by all working members of the household during the previous calendar year.
- **Family Real Estate Assets:** Square footage and value of the family residential property or rental agreements.
- **Moveable Assets:** Bank balances, deposits, and savings accounts held by all household members as of December 31 of the previous tax year.

### The Financial Threshold
To be eligible for the full scholarship package (Category A / Maximum Bracket):
- Your family's **ISEE Parificato must typically be below €24,000 to €27,000** (depending on the region's annual call for applications).
- For international students coming from developing or non-OECD countries, typical local salaries when converted to Euros usually fall well below this €24,000 threshold, placing the vast majority of applicants in the highest funding tier.

## 3. Required Documentation for the ISEE Parificato

Because regional agencies inspect foreign financial records closely, document preparation is the most critical phase of the Italian scholarship journey:

1. **Family Status Certificate (Stato di Famiglia):** An official civil registry document listing all members residing in your family household, including their relationship to the applicant.
2. **Annual Income Declaration:** Official tax certificates, employment earnings summaries, or pension slips for all working household members for the previous calendar year.
3. **Real Estate Property Certificate:** Official documentation indicating the ownership of the family home, including total surface area in square meters. If the family rents, a certified copy of the rental lease contract is required.
4. **Bank Balances & Financial Assets:** Official bank statements showing balances and average balances for all family accounts as of December 31.
5. **Apostille or Consular Legalization:** All documents issued in your home country must receive an official Apostille (for Hague Convention countries) or consular legalization at the local Italian Embassy/Consulate.
6. **Certified Sworn Italian Translations:** All documents must be translated into Italian by an accredited, certified translator or registered court translator (traduzione giurata). ## 4. Application Timeline & Regional Portal Submission

The application process follows a synchronized annual schedule:

- **May to June:** Universities publish course admission decisions and open pre-enrollment on the Universitaly portal.
- **June to July:** Regional scholarship agencies (ER.GO, DSU, EDISU, LazioDisco) publish their annual Calls for Application (Bando di Concorso).
- **July to August:** Applicants submit their online scholarship application forms on the respective regional agency portals, uploading their translated and legalized ISEE Parificato dossier.
- **September to October:** Provisional ranking lists (graduatorie provvisorie) are published, followed by final ranking lists (graduatorie definitive).
- **November to December:** First cash stipend disbursement is transferred to the student's Italian bank account.

## 5. Visa Proof of Funds & Banking Logistics

While regional scholarships provide substantial funding upon arrival, Italian embassies strictly require applicants to show **visa proof of financial sustenance** during their National Student Visa (Type D) interview:
- The Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs requires proof of at least **€467.65 per month** (€6,080 per academic year).
- Even with a pending scholarship application, consular officers expect to see personal or parental bank statements covering this amount before stamping your visa.
- In addition, paying international pre-enrollment fees, translation charges, and transcript dispatches requires reliable cross-border banking.
