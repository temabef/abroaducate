The Netherlands is recognized as the pioneer of English-taught higher education in continental Europe. With over **2,100 accredited degree programs taught 100% in English**, world-renowned research institutions (such as TU Delft, University of Amsterdam, Wageningen University, and Erasmus University Rotterdam), and a 95% English-speaking population, the Netherlands attracts over 120,000 international students annually.

Furthermore, international graduates in the Netherlands benefit from the **Orientation Year (*Zoekjaar*) residence permit**, which grants non-EU graduates one full year to seek employment or launch an innovative startup in the Dutch economy.

However, for non-EU/EEA international students, Dutch university tuition fees range from **€8,000 to €20,000 per year** depending on the discipline.

To ease this financial investment, the Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, together with Dutch research universities and universities of applied sciences, funds the prestigious **NL Scholarship** (formerly known as the *Holland Scholarship*).

Below, we detail how the NL Scholarship works, explain how to navigate the centralized national application portal (**Studielink**), compare Research Universities vs. Universities of Applied Sciences (UAS), and outline step-by-step submission instructions.

## 1. The NL Scholarship (Holland Scholarship) Explained

![Study desk flatlay with Dutch Studielink portal on laptop, university admission letter, transcripts, and coffee]({{BODY_IMAGE_URL}})

The **NL Scholarship** is financed by the Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science and participating Dutch higher education institutions:

- **Financial Award:** A one-off cash grant of **€5,000** awarded during your first year of study to help offset living costs and tuition.
- **Target Audience:** Non-EU/EEA international students applying for a full-time Bachelor's or Master's degree at a participating Dutch university.
- **Combined Scholarships:** Many Dutch research universities (such as TU Delft Excellence Scholarships or University of Amsterdam Merit Scholarships) allow recipients to pair the NL Scholarship with institutional tuition fee waivers, creating comprehensive financial packages.

### Participating Institutions
Virtually all top-tier Dutch research universities (WO) and universities of applied sciences (HBO) participate in the NL Scholarship scheme, including:
- Delft University of Technology (TU Delft)
- University of Amsterdam (UvA)
- Utrecht University
- Erasmus University Rotterdam
- Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e)
- Maastricht University
- Hanze University of Applied Sciences

## 2. Research Universities (WO) vs. Universities of Applied Sciences (HBO)

The Dutch higher education system features a dual structure that international applicants must understand:

1. **Research Universities (*Wetenschappelijk Onderwijs - WO*):**
   - **Academic Focus:** Independent theoretical research, analytical methodology, and academic rigor.
   - **Degree Structure:** Standard 1-year (Business, Humanities) or 2-year (STEM, Engineering) Master of Science degrees.
   - **Ideal Candidate:** Students aiming for PhD tracks, corporate R&D, or analytical leadership roles.
2. **Universities of Applied Sciences (*Hoger Beroepsonderwijs - HBO*):**
   - **Academic Focus:** Practical, industry-aligned training, mandatory corporate internships, and applied project work.
   - **Degree Structure:** Professional Master's degrees directly tied to market skillsets.
   - **Advantage:** Lower tuition fees and higher direct employer hiring rates upon graduation.

## 3. Navigating Studielink & The Application Process

All applications to Dutch universities begin through the centralized digital portal, **Studielink.nl**:

### Step 1: Create a Studielink Account
- Create your account without a DigiD (which is reserved for Dutch residents).
- Select your target university and specific English-taught degree program.
- You can submit up to **four active program applications** simultaneously on Studielink.

### Step 2: Access the University's Internal Application System (Osiris / Canvas)
- Once your application is logged in Studielink, your chosen university emails you login credentials for their internal admissions system (often powered by Osiris or Embark).
- Upload your academic dossier, resume, statement of purpose, and passport scan.

### Step 3: Check the Scholarship Application Box
- Within the university's internal portal, navigate to the financial aid section. Select **"Apply for the NL Scholarship"** and submit your supplemental motivation essay before the institutional deadline (typically **February 1** or **May 1**).

## 4. Required Documentation Checklist

Dutch university admissions committees conduct thorough reviews of academic transcripts and syllabus alignment:

1. **Certified Academic Transcripts:** Scanned copies of your complete undergraduate semester mark sheets. If your records were issued in a language other than English or Dutch, you must provide certified translations. You can secure official certified academic translations meeting Dutch immigration and university standards via [ImmiTranslate](https://immitranslate.com/?ref=543dj3rd).
2. **Course Descriptions / Module Syllabus:** Engineering and quantitative programs often require detailed course descriptions of your mathematics, programming, and core subjects to verify ECTS credit equivalence.
3. **Motivation Letter:** A rigorous 1 to 2-page essay explaining your academic foundations, research focus, and reasons for choosing the Dutch curriculum. You can craft and refine your motivation letter with the [Abroaducate SOP Builder](/sop).
4. **Letters of Recommendation:** Typically 2 letters from academic professors.
5. **English Proficiency Scores:** IELTS Academic (minimum 6.5 to 7.0) or TOEFL iBT (minimum 90 to 100). Many Dutch universities also accept official Medium of Instruction (MOI) letters from English-speaking countries.

## 5. Dutch Student Housing & Cross-Border Money Transfers

Finding student accommodation in the Netherlands is notoriously competitive, and universities do not guarantee housing. Dutch student cities like Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Delft, and Utrecht experience low rental vacancies.

It is strongly advised to begin searching for housing at least four to six months before your program begins.
