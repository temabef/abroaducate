For prospective international students across Africa, South Asia, and the Caribbean, one of the most contentious requirements of the study-abroad application process is the mandatory standardized English proficiency exam.

Students who have completed four to five years of undergraduate education conducted entirely in the English language are frequently shocked when European, North American, or Australian universities demand that they pay **$220 to $260** for an IELTS Academic or TOEFL iBT test.

For many applicants, this requirement feels both financially redundant and academically dismissive.

What many applicants do not realize is that hundreds of prestigious international universities and consular visa offices officially accept an alternative institutional document: the **English Medium of Instruction (MOI) Letter** (often referred to as an *English Proficiency Certificate* or *Language of Instruction Verification*).

When properly drafted, officially signed by your university registrar, and authenticated with institutional seals, **an MOI letter can completely exempt you from taking IELTS or TOEFL**, saving you hundreds of dollars in testing expenses and eliminating weeks of testing delays.

Below, we detail what an MOI letter is, the exact legal wording your university registrar must include, the countries and universities that accept it, and how to use it for admissions and student visa processing.

---

## 1. What is an English Medium of Instruction (MOI) Letter?

An English Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter is an official document issued by the Academic Registrar, Dean of Academic Affairs, or Examination Office of your previous university.

It provides legal attestation that:
1. You were officially enrolled in and graduated from an accredited post-secondary degree program.
2. The entire curriculum—including lectures, practical laboratory sessions, assignments, textbooks, and examinations—was conducted **100% in the English language**.
3. Your communicative, analytical, and academic fluency in English meets the standard required for advanced postgraduate study.

---

## 2. Which Countries & Universities Accept MOI Letters?

![Study desk flatlay with official English Medium of Instruction certificate, registrar stamps, transcripts, and pen]({{BODY_IMAGE_URL}})

Acceptance of an MOI letter depends on the destination country, the specific university, and the immigration guidelines of the host consulate:

### 1. Germany
Germany is the most prominent European destination accepting MOI letters:
- **Public Universities:** The vast majority of English-taught Master of Science and Master of Engineering programs at German public universities (such as TU Munich, RWTH Aachen, University of Stuttgart, and FAU Erlangen-Nuremberg) explicitly state in their admissions regulations that applicants whose previous degree was taught in English can satisfy language requirements by submitting an official MOI letter.
- **Visa Processing:** German Consulates and VFS Global accept an official MOI letter or university admission letter confirming language proficiency for the National Student Visa (§ 16b AufenthG).

### 2. France
- For English-taught Master's programs at French public universities and *Grandes Écoles*, an official MOI letter is widely accepted during Campus France (*Études en France*) pre-consular reviews and university admissions.

### 3. Poland & Italy
- Public universities in Poland (such as Warsaw University of Technology and University of Warsaw) and Italy (Politecnico di Milano, University of Padua) routinely accept MOI letters for non-EU students whose undergraduate degrees were completed in English-speaking nations or universities.

### 4. Select US & UK Universities
- In the United States and the United Kingdom, universities often grant automated English waivers to graduates of universities in countries where English is the official national language (e.g., Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Singapore, Jamaica) or where an MOI is verified through an external credential evaluation agency.

## 3. The Exact Registrar Wording (Official Template)

Many universities reject MOI letters because applicants submit vague statements written by departmental lecturers or student affairs coordinators.

**An MOI letter must be printed on official university letterhead, signed by the University Registrar or Director of Academic Affairs, and stamped with the university's official raised or wet-ink seal.**

Here is the exact structural wording required by international admissions boards:

```text
[OFFICIAL UNIVERSITY LETTERHEAD]
[Office of the Academic Registrar / Directorate of Academic Affairs]
[University Address, Official Email, Website, Phone Number]

Date: [DD/MM/YYYY]
Reference Number: [Official Reference/File No.]

TO WHOM IT MAY CONCERN

CERTIFICATE OF ENGLISH MEDIUM OF INSTRUCTION

This is to certify that [Full Legal Name of Student], Matriculation/Student ID Number: [Student ID Number], was a bonafide student of [Official Name of University], enrolled in the [Name of Department/Faculty].

[He/She] successfully completed the prescribed curriculum and was awarded the degree of [Exact Degree Title, e.g., Bachelor of Science in Mechanical Engineering] with [Degree Classification, e.g., First Class Honours / Second Class Honours Upper Division] on [Date of Graduation].

We hereby officially confirm that the sole Medium of Instruction, teaching, examination, and laboratory coursework for the entirety of the aforementioned academic degree program was the ENGLISH LANGUAGE. All lectures, textbooks, research projects, assignments, and written/oral examinations were delivered and evaluated exclusively in English.

[Student's Full Name] has demonstrated excellent proficiency, verbal fluency, and written competence in the English language throughout [his/her] period of study.

This certificate is issued at the request of the graduate for postgraduate admissions, visa processing, and official academic purposes.

Yours faithfully,

[Official Registrar's Signature]
___________________________________
[Full Name of the Academic Registrar]
Academic Registrar / Controller of Examinations
[Official Stamp / Embossed University Seal]
```

## 4. How to Prevent Rejection of Your MOI Letter

To ensure your MOI letter passes admissions inspection without administrative delays:

1. **Issued by Central Administration:** Letters signed by course lecturers, student union presidents, or academic advisors are routinely discarded. The document must originate from the central Registrar's office.
2. **Include Contact Verification:** Admissions committees frequently send email verifications to the registrar's official institutional domain (e.g., `registrar@university.edu`, not generic `@gmail.com` addresses). Ensure the letterhead contains an active official contact email.
3. **Certified Translations (If Applicable):** If your degree records, academic transcripts, or provisional certificates are in a bilingual format or a local language, you must include certified word-for-word translations. You can secure accredited translations accepted by international admissions committees through [ImmiTranslate](https://immitranslate.com/?ref=543dj3rd).
4. **Statement of Purpose Alignment:** In your academic Statement of Purpose, explicitly mention that your previous degree was conducted entirely in English, reinforcing your academic readiness. You can format and polish your essay using the [Abroaducate SOP Builder](/sop).
